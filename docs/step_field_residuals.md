# Spatially-varying phase-step residuals in AIA

This document derives, in closed form, the bias that a **spatially-varying phase-step error**
puts into the fields recovered by the piston-model AIA solve, starting from the per-frame model
of [`interference_model.md`](interference_model.md) and the pixel-step solve of
[`aia.md`](aia.md). It does not re-derive either document; both are cited by equation number.
The companion document [`frame_moments.md`](frame_moments.md) is used the same way, for its
frame-moment notation ($R$, $R^{(2)}$, $\langle g^2\rangle$).

The derivation is pointwise in $(x,y)$ and holds for *any* per-frame field, so the pure linear
tilt $\Delta_n(x,y) = k_{xn}x+k_{yn}y$ is treated throughout as one special case ($M=1$) of the
general truncated-Taylor step field, not as a separate model.

`interference_model.md` assumption 3 records that this setup's parasitic actuator tilt was
measured experimentally negligible, justifying the spatially-uniform piston $\delta(t)$ that
every other document in this package is built on. This document is what to fall back on when
that measurement turns out not to hold at the level of today's target accuracy — it does not
contradict assumption 3 so much as quantify the cost of small departures from it.

As in `frame_moments.md`, the $(x,y)$ dependence of $a$, $b$, $\Phi$ (and, here, of the step-field
coefficients) is suppressed for readability; every equation holds pixel-by-pixel unless stated
otherwise.

## 1. Setup

### 1.1 The general step field

`aia.md` Eq. (1) (equivalently `interference_model.md` Eq. 17) assumes the phase step is a pure
piston, $\delta_n$, common to the whole field. Suppose instead the true step carries a small
spatially-varying error on top of the piston, expanded as a truncated Taylor series about the
field centroid to total degree $M$:

$$\delta_n(x,y) = \delta_n + \Delta_n(x,y), \qquad
\Delta_n(x,y) = \sum_{j=1}^{J} c_{jn}\,p_j(x,y) \tag{T1}$$

where $p_1,\dots,p_J$ are the field's monomials of total degree $1$ through $M$ ($x$, $y$, $x^2$,
$xy$, $y^2$, …, so $J=\tfrac{(M+1)(M+2)}{2}-1$), and $c_{jn}$ is frame $n$'s coefficient of basis
term $j$. Degree $0$ (the constant) is deliberately excluded from the basis — that is exactly the
piston $\delta_n$, already accounted for separately. $M=1$ (so $p_1=x,\ p_2=y$,
$c_{1n}=k_{xn}$, $c_{2n}=k_{yn}$) is the pure linear tilt; $M=0$ gives $J=0$ — no basis terms at
all, $\Delta_n(x,y)\equiv0$ — the piston-only model this whole document is a correction to.

The true per-frame intensity is

$$I_n(x,y) = a(x,y) + g_n\,b(x,y)\cos\big(\Phi(x,y) + \delta_n + \Delta_n(x,y)\big) \tag{T2}$$

The piston-model AIA solve (`aia.md` §"Pixel step") is fit to data generated by Eq. (T2) while
its design matrix $A$ (`aia.md` Eq. 5) is built from the piston $\delta_n$ alone — $\Delta_n$
does not appear in the model the solver assumes. This document computes the resulting bias in
the recovered $(a, u, v)$ and, from them, $(a, b, \Phi)$, then (§8) derives an estimator that
recovers $\{c_{jn}\}$ from the AIA residual.

Everywhere below, $g_n$ is treated as a known value at the point the linearization is taken
around — this derivation does not care whether it came from a separate measurement or from
`aia.md`'s jointly-fitted `fit_gain` path. `phase.methods.step_field.aia_step_field` re-derives
$g_n$ (and the AIA pixel/frame step it re-runs each refinement round) from the current
step-field-corrected data when `fit_gain=True`, so $g_n$ can change between refinement rounds;
each round's $\{c_{jn}\}$ estimate (§8) is still computed against that round's own $(a,u,v,g)$,
exactly as this document assumes.

### 1.2 Origin and gauge conventions

Two independent conventions make the decomposition of Eq. (T1) unambiguous — one spatial, one
temporal.

**(i) Origin at the field centroid, each $p_j$ zero-mean over the field.** Place the origin of
$(x,y)$ at the field centroid and choose each basis function to have zero spatial average,

$$\big\langle p_j(x,y)\big\rangle_{x,y} = 0, \qquad j=1,\dots,J \tag{T3}$$

so that $\big\langle \Delta_n(x,y)\big\rangle_{x,y} = 0$ for every $n$. This is what makes
$\delta_n$ well-defined as *the* spatial mean of the true
step and $\Delta_n$ its zero-mean remainder: for $M=1$, $p_1=x,\ p_2=y$ already satisfy
Eq. (T3) once the origin sits at the centroid; for $M\ge2$ each higher-order monomial must be
mean-subtracted explicitly (e.g. $p=x^2-\langle x^2\rangle_{x,y}$), since $\langle x^2\rangle_{x,y}$
is the field's variance in $x$ and is not zero just because $\langle x\rangle_{x,y}=0$. Note this
zero-mean condition is a constraint on the *field average*, not on the value at the centroid
itself — $x^2-\langle x^2\rangle_{x,y}$ is generally nonzero at $x=0$. Only the pure degree-1
terms vanish pointwise at the centroid; §5.6 returns to why this distinction matters for reading
a residual map.

**(ii) Frame-mean-zero coefficients.** Require, in addition,

$$\big\langle c_{jn}\big\rangle_n = 0, \qquad j=1,\dots,J \tag{T3b}$$

Unlike (i), this is not a labeling convention but tracks an *exact* invariance of Eq. (T2):
shifting every $c_{jn} \to c_{jn}-\bar c_j$ while adding $\bar c_j\,p_j(x,y)$ to $\Phi(x,y)$
leaves $\delta_n+\Delta_n+\Phi$, and hence $I_n$, unchanged for every $n$ and every pixel — the
static part of a step-field coefficient is indistinguishable from a static addition to the
phase itself (a piece of $\phi_{\text{carrier}}$, `interference_model.md` Eq. 15). Eq. (T3b) is
what pins that ambiguity, the spatial analogue of `aia.md`'s phase-origin pin $\delta_0=0$ for
the piston. For $M=1$ this is exactly why a frame-independent tilt is harmless (§9.3); §8.4 shows
it is needed to define the estimator uniquely for any $M\ge1$, because a per-frame least-squares
fit (§8.1) has no way to enforce Eq. (T3b) on its own.

### 1.3 Scope: $\delta_n$ is not the unknown here

$\delta_n$ itself is not assumed wrong here — only the field error $\Delta_n$ is unmodeled.
Concretely: this derivation propagates $\Delta_n$'s
effect through the **pixel step** (`aia.md` §"Pixel step") at a fixed, correct piston
$\delta_n$ — the same linear solve $A^\top A\,(a,u,v)^\top = A^\top I$ that step already
performs, unmodified. The companion question — whether $\Delta_n$ also biases the **frame
step**'s own estimate $\hat\delta_n$ (`aia.md` Eq. 6–8), so that the piston fed into the pixel
step is itself already off — is addressed separately in Section 7, and shown there to be a
strictly smaller, second-order effect under the same good-coverage condition `aia.md` already
invokes for $\kappa_{ps}$, though one that degrades as $M$ grows. Sections 2–6 therefore hold
$\delta_n$ fixed at its true value.

## 2. Linearization of the perturbed model

Nothing in this section uses the spatial shape of $\Delta_n$ — it holds pointwise at every
$(x,y)$ for whatever value $\Delta_n$ takes there, whether that value comes from a pure tilt or
from the general expansion of Eq. (T1). The polynomial structure only enters once these
per-pixel results are combined across pixels (§3 onward).

### 2.1 Quadrature shorthand for the design-matrix rows

`aia.md` Eq. (5) builds $A$ from rows $[1,\ g_n\cos\delta_n,\ g_n\sin\delta_n]$. Write

$$c_n \equiv g_n\cos\delta_n, \qquad s_n \equiv g_n\sin\delta_n \tag{T4}$$

so the noise-free piston prediction (`aia.md` Eq. 3) is $I_n^{(0)} = a + c_n u + s_n v$, with $u,
v$ the quadrature fields of `aia.md` Eq. (2). (Note $c_n$ here is the quadrature coefficient of
`aia.md`, unrelated to the step-field coefficients $c_{jn}$ of Eq. T1 — both names are inherited
from their respective source documents and kept as-is to avoid renaming either.)

### 2.2 Expanding the cosine in $\Delta_n$

Expand Eq. (T2)'s cosine using $\cos(\theta+\Delta) = \cos\theta\cos\Delta - \sin\theta\sin\Delta$
with $\theta = \Phi+\delta_n$, then Taylor-expand $\cos\Delta_n \approx 1 - \tfrac12\Delta_n^2$
and $\sin\Delta_n \approx \Delta_n$ (dropping $O(\Delta_n^3)$, consistent with keeping terms
through second order):

$$\cos(\Phi+\delta_n+\Delta_n) \approx \cos(\Phi+\delta_n) - \Delta_n\sin(\Phi+\delta_n) - \tfrac12\Delta_n^2\cos(\Phi+\delta_n) \tag{T5}$$

Substituting into Eq. (T2) and collecting orders in $\Delta_n$, $I_n = I_n^{(0)} + \Delta I_n^{(1)} + \Delta I_n^{(2)} + O(\Delta_n^3)$ with

$$\Delta I_n^{(1)} = -g_n b\,\Delta_n\sin(\Phi+\delta_n), \qquad
\Delta I_n^{(2)} = -\tfrac12 g_n b\,\Delta_n^2\cos(\Phi+\delta_n) \tag{T6}$$

### 2.3 Converting to quadrature variables

Expand $\sin(\Phi+\delta_n) = \sin\Phi\cos\delta_n+\cos\Phi\sin\delta_n$ and use
$b\cos\Phi = u$, $b\sin\Phi = -v$ (from $u=b\cos\Phi$, $v=-b\sin\Phi$):

$$g_nb\sin(\Phi+\delta_n) = g_n\big[(-v)\cos\delta_n + u\sin\delta_n\big] = u\,s_n - v\,c_n$$

so, with the same substitution in $\Delta I_n^{(2)}$ ($g_nb\cos(\Phi+\delta_n) = u\,c_n+v\,s_n$,
by the same steps with $\cos\Phi,\sin\Phi$ in place of $\sin\Phi,\cos\Phi$):

$$\Delta I_n^{(1)} = \Delta_n\,(v\,c_n - u\,s_n), \qquad
\Delta I_n^{(2)} = -\tfrac12\Delta_n^2\,(u\,c_n + v\,s_n) \tag{T7}$$

Define $w_n \equiv u\,s_n - v\,c_n = -(v\,c_n-u\,s_n)$, so Eq. (T7) reads
$\Delta I_n^{(1)} = -w_n\,\Delta_n$; $w_n = \partial I_n^{(0)}/\partial(-\delta_n)$ is exactly the
sensitivity of the noise-free piston prediction to a step-timing shift at frame $n$
(differentiate $I_n^{(0)}=a+c_nu+s_nv$ through $c_n,s_n$). $w_n$ reappears as the regressor of the
estimator in §8, and its derivative-of-a-cosine-is-a-sine origin is the structural reason every
bias below comes out in quadrature with the corresponding `frame_moments.md` leakage term.

## 3. Step-field-weighted frame moments

Define, as the exact analogues of `frame_moments.md` Eqs. (F2)–(F5) but weighted by the step
field $\Delta_n$ (a field-dependent, zero-frame-mean quantity — Eq. T3b — not a constant, so
these residuals are themselves functions of $(x,y)$):

$$R_\Delta = \big\langle g_n\Delta_n\,e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n, \qquad
\langle g^2\Delta\rangle = \big\langle g_n^2\Delta_n\big\rangle_n, \qquad
R_\Delta^{(2)} = \big\langle g_n^2\Delta_n\,e^{i2\delta_n}\big\rangle_n \tag{T8}$$

with $\psi_\Delta = \arg R_\Delta$, $\psi_{\Delta2} = \arg R_\Delta^{(2)}$, and real/imaginary
parts $R_{\Delta} = R_{\Delta,c}+iR_{\Delta,s}$, $R_\Delta^{(2)} = R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}+iR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}$
(matching `frame_moments.md`'s $R_c,R_s,R_c^{(2)},R_s^{(2)}$ convention). Since $\Delta_n$ is
itself linear in the coefficients $c_{jn}$ (Eq. T1) and frame-averaging is linear, each of these
fields decomposes exactly into the fixed spatial basis:

$$R_\Delta(x,y) = \sum_{j=1}^J \rho_j\,p_j(x,y), \quad \rho_j \equiv \big\langle g_n c_{jn}e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n; \qquad
\langle g^2\Delta\rangle(x,y) = \sum_{j=1}^J \mu_j\,p_j(x,y), \quad \mu_j \equiv \big\langle g_n^2 c_{jn}\big\rangle_n \tag{T8a}$$

and likewise $R_\Delta^{(2)}(x,y) = \sum_j \rho_j^{(2)}p_j(x,y)$ with
$\rho_j^{(2)} = \langle g_n^2 c_{jn}e^{i2\delta_n}\rangle_n$ — a set of $J$ complex (or, for
$\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$, real) numbers, one per basis term, computable directly from the
realized $\{c_{jn}\}$ and $\{\delta_n,g_n\}$ sequences (two numbers, $k_x,k_y$-weighted, at
$M=1$).

Section 6 needs the second-order-in-$\Delta_n$ counterparts. Since $\Delta_n^2 = \sum_{j,j'}
c_{jn}c_{j'n}\,p_jp_{j'}$ is quadratic in the coefficients, these decompose into the basis's
*pairwise products* instead:

$$S(x,y) = \big\langle g_n\Delta_n^2\,e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n = \sum_{j,j'}\sigma_{jj'}\,p_jp_{j'}, \quad
\sigma_{jj'} \equiv \big\langle g_n c_{jn}c_{j'n}e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n \tag{T9}$$

and likewise $S^{(2)}(x,y)=\sum_{jj'}\sigma_{jj'}^{(2)}p_jp_{j'}$,
$S^{(0)}(x,y)=\sum_{jj'}\sigma_{jj'}^{(0)}p_jp_{j'}$ with
$\sigma_{jj'}^{(0)}=\langle g_n^2c_{jn}c_{j'n}\rangle_n$. $\Sigma^{(0)}\equiv[\sigma_{jj'}^{(0)}]$
is the frame-average of the rank-1 matrices $g_n^2\,c_{\cdot n}c_{\cdot n}^\top$, each positive
semi-definite, so $\Sigma^{(0)}$ itself is PSD; consequently $S^{(0)}(x,y) = p(x,y)^\top
\Sigma^{(0)}p(x,y) \ge 0$ at every pixel — ordinary PSD-quadratic-form positivity, matching
$\Delta_n^2\ge0,\ g_n^2\ge0$ directly. With $\psi_S=\arg S$, $\psi_{S2}=\arg S^{(2)}$ as before.

## 4. Propagating the perturbation through the pixel step

### 4.1 The pixel step as an exact linear solve

`aia.md` Eq. (5) gives the design matrix $A$ (rows $[1,c_n,s_n]$); the pixel step solves
$(a,u,v)^\top = (A^\top A)^{-1}A^\top I$ at every pixel, i.e. $(a,u,v)^\top = A_p^{-1}A^\top I$
with $A_p \equiv A^\top A$ (`aia.md`'s pixel-step normal matrix — the matrix the derivation
brief calls $M$). Because $I_n^{(0)} = a+c_nu+s_nv$ lies exactly in the column space of $A$ by
construction, $A_p^{-1}A^\top I^{(0)} = (a,u,v)^\top$ exactly, so for $I = I^{(0)}+\Delta I$ the
solve returns

$$(\hat a,\hat u,\hat v)^\top = (a,u,v)^\top + A_p^{-1}A^\top\Delta I
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(\Delta a,\Delta u,\Delta v)^\top \equiv A_p^{-1}A^\top\Delta I \tag{T10}$$

by linearity of least squares — the same solve `aia.md` already runs, with no re-derivation of
the pseudoinverse itself. (Measurement noise adds its own, statistically independent term to
Eq. (T10); it is not carried here, since this document isolates the deterministic bias.) This
step is pointwise in $(x,y)$ and never uses the spatial shape of $\Delta_n$.

### 4.2 Componentwise right-hand side

Using $\Delta I_n^{(1)}$ from Eq. (T7), $A^\top\Delta I^{(1)} = N\big(\langle\Delta
I_n^{(1)}\rangle_n,\ \langle c_n\Delta I_n^{(1)}\rangle_n,\ \langle s_n\Delta
I_n^{(1)}\rangle_n\big)^\top$. The first entry is immediate:

$$\big\langle\Delta I_n^{(1)}\big\rangle_n = v\langle c_n\Delta_n\rangle_n - u\langle s_n\Delta_n\rangle_n = vR_{\Delta,c} - uR_{\Delta,s}$$

The other two need $\langle c_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n$, $\langle s_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n$,
$\langle c_ns_n\Delta_n\rangle_n$. Using $c_n^2=\tfrac12g_n^2(1+\cos2\delta_n)$,
$s_n^2=\tfrac12g_n^2(1-\cos2\delta_n)$, $c_ns_n=\tfrac12g_n^2\sin2\delta_n$ (standard
double-angle identities, exactly as `aia.md`/`frame_moments.md` use for $\kappa_{ps}$ and $R^{(2)}$):

$$\langle c_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n = \tfrac12\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + \tfrac12R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}, \qquad
\langle s_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n = \tfrac12\langle g^2\Delta\rangle - \tfrac12R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}, \qquad
\langle c_ns_n\Delta_n\rangle_n = \tfrac12R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}$$

so

$$\big\langle c_n\Delta I_n^{(1)}\big\rangle_n = \tfrac12v\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + \tfrac12\big(vR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}-uR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}\big), \qquad
\big\langle s_n\Delta I_n^{(1)}\big\rangle_n = -\tfrac12u\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + \tfrac12\big(vR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}+uR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}\big) \tag{T11}$$

### 4.3 The pixel-step normal matrix in these moments

The same double-angle identities give $A_p/N$ itself in `frame_moments.md`'s own moments $R$,
$R^{(2)}$ (its Eqs. F2–F5), by the identical computation with $\Delta_n\to1$:

$$\frac{A_p}{N} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & R_c & R_s \\ R_c & \tfrac12(\langle g^2\rangle+R_c^{(2)}) & \tfrac12R_s^{(2)} \\ R_s & \tfrac12R_s^{(2)} & \tfrac12(\langle g^2\rangle-R_c^{(2)}) \end{pmatrix} \tag{T12}$$

confirming $A_p$ is exactly `aia.md`'s pixel-step normal matrix, expressed in the moments
already named in `frame_moments.md`. Inverting Eq. (T12) in general is possible but not
illuminating; the acquisitions this package targets are run at the good-coverage condition
`aia.md` already recommends for a low $\kappa_p$ — uniform steps and constant $g_n$, for which
`frame_moments.md` §2.4 gives $R=R^{(2)}=0$ exactly ($N\ge3$). Eq. (T12) then diagonalizes,

$$\frac{A_p}{N} = \operatorname{diag}\!\Big(1,\ \tfrac12\langle g^2\rangle,\ \tfrac12\langle g^2\rangle\Big)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_p^{-1} = \frac1N\operatorname{diag}\!\Big(1,\ \tfrac{2}{\langle g^2\rangle},\ \tfrac{2}{\langle g^2\rangle}\Big) \tag{T13}$$

and this is the $A_p^{-1}$ used from here on. (A poorly-conditioned, non-uniform step set biases
$\Delta a,\Delta u,\Delta v$ through the off-diagonal terms of Eq. (T12) as well as through the
step-field moments of Eq. (T8) — a second, compounding source of the large-$\kappa_p$
inaccuracy `aia.md`'s diagnostics already warn about, not analyzed further here.)

Combining Eqs. (T10), (T11), (T13):

$$\Delta a = vR_{\Delta,c} - uR_{\Delta,s}, \qquad
\Delta u = \frac{v\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + vR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}-uR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}}{\langle g^2\rangle}, \qquad
\Delta v = \frac{-u\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + vR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}+uR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}}{\langle g^2\rangle} \tag{T14}$$

## 5. First-order bias in $a$, $b$, $\Phi$

### 5.1 A quadrature lemma

$b=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$, $\Phi=\operatorname{atan2}(-v,u)$ (`aia.md` Eq. 9) give, by direct
differentiation ($db = (u\,du+v\,dv)/b$; $d\Phi$ from $d\,\operatorname{atan2}(Y,X) =
(X\,dY-Y\,dX)/(X^2+Y^2)$ with $Y=-v,X=u$),

$$\Delta b = \cos\Phi\,\Delta u - \sin\Phi\,\Delta v, \qquad
\Delta\Phi = \frac{-\sin\Phi\,\Delta u - \cos\Phi\,\Delta v}{b} \tag{T15}$$

valid to first order in $(\Delta u,\Delta v)$. Substituting $u=b\cos\Phi,\ v=-b\sin\Phi$ gives
four identities used repeatedly below and in Section 6:

$$u\cos\Phi - v\sin\Phi = b, \qquad u\cos\Phi + v\sin\Phi = b\cos2\Phi, \qquad
v\cos\Phi + u\sin\Phi = 0, \qquad v\cos\Phi - u\sin\Phi = -b\sin2\Phi \tag{T16}$$

(each a one-line check with $\cos^2\Phi\pm\sin^2\Phi$ or $2\sin\Phi\cos\Phi$).

### 5.2 $\Delta a$ — first harmonic

From Eq. (T14), $\Delta a = vR_{\Delta,c}-uR_{\Delta,s}$. Writing $R_{\Delta,c}=|R_\Delta|\cos\psi_\Delta$,
$R_{\Delta,s}=|R_\Delta|\sin\psi_\Delta$ and substituting $u=b\cos\Phi,\ v=-b\sin\Phi$:

$$\Delta a = -b|R_\Delta|(\sin\Phi\cos\psi_\Delta+\cos\Phi\sin\psi_\Delta) = -b\,|R_\Delta|\sin(\Phi+\psi_\Delta) = -b\,\operatorname{Im}\!\big[R_\Delta e^{i\Phi}\big] \tag{T17}$$

### 5.3 $\Delta b$ — second harmonic

Substitute $\Delta u,\Delta v$ (Eq. T14) into $\Delta b=\cos\Phi\,\Delta u-\sin\Phi\,\Delta v$:

$$\langle g^2\rangle\,\Delta b = \langle g^2\Delta\rangle\,(v\cos\Phi+u\sin\Phi)
+ R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}(v\cos\Phi-u\sin\Phi) - R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}(u\cos\Phi+v\sin\Phi)$$

By Eq. (T16) the first bracket is $0$ — **the step-field-weighted carrier moment $\langle
g^2\Delta\rangle$ drops out of $\Delta b$ entirely** — and the remaining two collapse via
Eq. (T16) to $-b\big[R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}\sin2\Phi+R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}\cos2\Phi\big] =
-b|R_\Delta^{(2)}|\sin(2\Phi+\psi_{\Delta2})$, so

$$\Delta b = -\frac{b\,|R_\Delta^{(2)}|}{\langle g^2\rangle}\sin(2\Phi+\psi_{\Delta2}) = -\frac{b}{\langle g^2\rangle}\operatorname{Im}\!\big[R_\Delta^{(2)}e^{i2\Phi}\big] \tag{T18}$$

### 5.4 $\Delta\Phi$ — a ramp plus a second harmonic

Substitute the same $\Delta u,\Delta v$ into $\Delta\Phi = (-\sin\Phi\,\Delta u-\cos\Phi\,\Delta v)/b$
and collect by $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$, $R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}$, $R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}$ exactly as in
§5.3. The coefficients that come out of the expansion are, by Eq. (T16), $u\cos\Phi-v\sin\Phi=b$
(for $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$), $-(u\cos\Phi+v\sin\Phi)=-b\cos2\Phi$ (for $R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}$),
and $u\sin\Phi-v\cos\Phi=b\sin2\Phi$ (for $R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}$):

$$\langle g^2\rangle\,b\,\Delta\Phi = b\langle g^2\Delta\rangle - b\big[R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}\cos2\Phi - R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}\sin2\Phi\big] = b\langle g^2\Delta\rangle - b\,|R_\Delta^{(2)}|\cos(2\Phi+\psi_{\Delta2})$$

so

$$\Delta\Phi = \frac{\langle g^2\Delta\rangle}{\langle g^2\rangle} - \frac{|R_\Delta^{(2)}|}{\langle g^2\rangle}\cos(2\Phi+\psi_{\Delta2})
= \frac{\langle g^2\Delta\rangle}{\langle g^2\rangle} - \frac{1}{\langle g^2\rangle}\operatorname{Re}\!\big[R_\Delta^{(2)}e^{i2\Phi}\big] \tag{T19}$$

### 5.5 The harmonic fingerprint

Eqs. (T17)–(T19) confirm exactly the fingerprint anticipated: **$\Delta a$ oscillates at the
first harmonic of $\Phi$ ($|R_\Delta|$), $\Delta b$ at the second ($|R_\Delta^{(2)}|$)** — the
same harmonic split `frame_moments.md` finds for the mean ($|R|$, Eq. F13) and variance
($|D|$, Eq. F21) leakage. The one structural difference: every bias here rides $\sin$
($\operatorname{Im}[\,\cdot\,]$), while `frame_moments.md`'s leakage terms ride $\cos$
($\operatorname{Re}[\,\cdot\,]$) — a direct consequence of Eq. (T7)'s $\Delta I_n^{(1)} =
-w_n\Delta_n$: a derivative of a cosine is a sine, so a step-field bias sits **in quadrature**
with a piston-timing bias of the same harmonic order. $\Delta\Phi$ additionally carries a
phase-independent ramp, $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle/\langle g^2\rangle$ — a spatially-smooth
addition to the recovered phase, of exactly the functional form of $\phi_{\text{carrier}}$
(`interference_model.md` Eq. 15), and removed the same way (carrier fitting/subtraction); it is
not a fringe artifact and is set aside from here on. None of Eqs. (T17)–(T19) used the spatial
shape of $\Delta_n$ — $|R_\Delta|,|R_\Delta^{(2)}|,\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$ are, per Eq. (T8a),
themselves degree-$M$ polynomials in $(x,y)$; how these envelopes vary with position is taken up
next.

### 5.6 The spatial envelope of each bias, and a diagnostic

By Eq. (T8a), $R_\Delta(x,y)=\sum_j\rho_j p_j(x,y)$ is a fixed linear combination of the same
degree-$1\ldots M$ basis used for $\Delta_n$ itself, and likewise for $R_\Delta^{(2)}$ and
$\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$. Three consequences, in increasing order of how directly they can be
checked against a real residual map:

1. **Zero field average, not a zero at the centroid.** Since every $p_j$ has zero field average
   (Eq. T3), so does $R_\Delta$ (and the other two envelopes): $\langle
   R_\Delta\rangle_{x,y}=0$. This is why $\Delta\Phi$'s ramp term (§5.4) is the only part of the
   bias with a genuine field-wide offset — none of the oscillatory envelopes can have one. It
   does *not* mean $R_\Delta(x,y)=0$ pointwise at the centroid for $M\ge2$: a curvature term
   contributes a bowl- or saddle-shaped envelope that is zero on average but generally nonzero,
   and often largest in magnitude, at the centroid itself.
2. **The pure-tilt term is the one exception.** For $j\in\{1,2\}$ ($p_1=x,p_2=y$), the envelope
   contribution is exactly zero at the centroid and grows linearly outward — the worked drift
   case of §9.4 describes exactly this: a bias that vanishes at the origin and grows with radius.
   A genuine quadratic-or-higher remainder in the true step field does not share this property;
   its envelope need not be smallest at the centroid.
3. **A usable diagnostic anyway.** Whether or not the envelope vanishes at the centroid, it is
   always a bounded, low-order (degree $\le M$, so degree $\le 2M$ for the second-order terms of
   §6) polynomial in $(x,y)$ — smooth and slowly varying compared to the fringe pattern itself.
   Consequently: a residual ripple whose RMS amplitude is essentially **uniform across the
   field**, independent of position, cannot be produced by *any* finite-degree $\Delta_n(x,y)$ —
   its cause lies elsewhere, most plausibly a phase-locked defect $\varepsilon(\Phi)$ from an
   imperfect frame model (`phase/ripple.py`'s `estimate_phase_ripple` targets exactly this). A
   residual that instead varies smoothly and systematically with position — larger away from the
   centroid, or with a visible bowl/saddle shape — is consistent with an unmodeled step-field
   remainder and is the signature to look for before increasing $M$.

## 6. Second order, where the first order vanishes

$\Delta b$ (Eq. T18) has no phase-independent term — a step-field error cannot show up as an
apparent loss of fringe visibility at first order. That effect appears only at second order in
$\Delta_n$, via $\Delta I_n^{(2)}$ (Eq. T7). Repeating §4.2's computation with $\Delta I_n^{(2)}$
in place of $\Delta I_n^{(1)}$ (same double-angle identities, now producing the second-order
moments of Eq. T9) gives

$$\Delta u^{(2)} = -\frac{uS^{(0)}+uS_c^{(2)}+vS_s^{(2)}}{2\langle g^2\rangle}, \qquad
\Delta v^{(2)} = -\frac{vS^{(0)}-vS_c^{(2)}+uS_s^{(2)}}{2\langle g^2\rangle} \tag{T20}$$

Substituting into $\Delta b^{(2)} = \cos\Phi\,\Delta u^{(2)}-\sin\Phi\,\Delta v^{(2)}$ and
grouping by $S^{(0)}$, $S_c^{(2)}$, $S_s^{(2)}$ exactly as in §5.3, the coefficients are, by
Eq. (T16), $u\cos\Phi-v\sin\Phi=b$ (for $S^{(0)}$), $u\cos\Phi+v\sin\Phi=b\cos2\Phi$ (for
$S_c^{(2)}$), and $v\cos\Phi-u\sin\Phi=-b\sin2\Phi$ (for $S_s^{(2)}$):

$$\Delta b^{(2)} = -\frac{b}{2\langle g^2\rangle}\Big[S^{(0)} + S_c^{(2)}\cos2\Phi - S_s^{(2)}\sin2\Phi\Big]
= -\frac{b}{2\langle g^2\rangle}\Big[S^{(0)} + |S^{(2)}|\cos(2\Phi+\psi_{S2})\Big] \tag{T21}$$

and, by the same substitution into $\Delta\Phi^{(2)} = (-\sin\Phi\,\Delta u^{(2)}-\cos\Phi\,\Delta v^{(2)})/b$
(coefficients $u\sin\Phi+v\cos\Phi=0$, $u\sin\Phi-v\cos\Phi=b\sin2\Phi$, $v\sin\Phi+u\cos\Phi=b\cos2\Phi$,
all from Eq. T16),

$$\Delta\Phi^{(2)} = \frac{S_c^{(2)}\sin2\Phi+S_s^{(2)}\cos2\Phi}{2\langle g^2\rangle} = \frac{|S^{(2)}|}{2\langle g^2\rangle}\sin(2\Phi+\psi_{S2}) \tag{T22}$$

Since $S^{(0)}(x,y)=p(x,y)^\top\Sigma^{(0)}p(x,y)\ge0$ always (§3), the phase-independent part of
Eq. (T21) is **strictly a visibility loss**, $-bS^{(0)}/(2\langle g^2\rangle) \le 0$. For a pure
tilt ($M=1$) $S^{(0)}\propto x^2+xy+y^2$ grows quadratically with distance from the field
centroid; for a general degree-$M$ step field $S^{(0)}$ is a degree-$2M$ polynomial (a quadratic
step field, $M=2$, gives a **quartic** visibility roll-off) — either way it mimics, in shape, a
genuine roll-off of the coherence envelope $\gamma(x,y)$ that `interference_model.md` Eq. (13)
already folds into $b(x,y)$: a step-field-induced $b$ roll-off and a real $\gamma$ roll-off are
not distinguishable from a single acquisition's recovered $b$ map alone.

$\Delta a$'s own second-order term, $\Delta a^{(2)} = -\tfrac12(uS_c+vS_s) =
-\tfrac{b}{2}\operatorname{Re}[Se^{i\Phi}]$ (same substitution, using $S=S_c+iS_s$ of Eq. T9),
is still purely first-harmonic — it corrects the size and phase of the same $\Delta a$ ripple
already present at first order (Eq. T17), rather than introducing a new harmonic. Unlike $b$ and
$\Phi$, $a$ therefore needs no second-order term to see a qualitatively new effect.

## 7. Why the frame step does not, to first order, re-absorb the step field

Section 1.3 deferred the question of whether $\Delta_n$ also biases the frame step's own
estimate $\hat\delta_n$ (`aia.md` Eq. 6–8), which would feed a wrong piston back into the pixel
step above. The frame step regresses each frame's $P=H\times W$ pixel values onto $[1,u,v]$
across the field (`aia.md` Eq. 6–7); the step field's contribution to frame $n$'s pixel values
is $\Delta I_n^{(1)}(x,y) = -w_n(x,y)\,\Delta_n(x,y)$ (Eq. T7), a term of degree $\le M$ in
$(x,y)$ (Eq. T1), while the regressors $u(x,y),v(x,y)$ carry the field's fringe structure. Under
the same good-coverage condition already invoked in Eq. (T13) — uniform steps, constant $g_n$,
and $\kappa_{ps}$ near its floor of 2 (`aia.md` §"Accuracy diagnostics") — $u,v$ are, by design,
spread as evenly as possible over the unit circle rather than correlated with any single
low-order spatial pattern, so the coupling is the projection of $\{w_n\,p_j\}_j$ onto
$\operatorname{span}\{1,u,v\}$, which stays weak for a low-order $p_j$: this is the same
field-coverage argument, applied spatially instead of temporally, that makes $R=R^{(2)}=0$ the
good-coverage condition in `frame_moments.md` §2.4. That coupling is not $M$-independent,
though: a higher-degree $p_j$ has more degrees of freedom to correlate with whatever smooth,
low-order structure $u,v$ already carry (e.g. a large sample-phase gradient, or the carrier
$\phi_{\text{carrier}}$ itself, `interference_model.md` Eq. 15) — the reason to keep $M$ small
(2–3) rather than push it arbitrarily high in the hope of soaking up more of the residual.
Quantifying that residual would require the frame step's own normal matrix $A_{ps}=B^\top B$
and is not worked out here; the pixel-step bias of Sections 4–6, computed at the true
(unshifted) $\delta_n$, is the leading-order effect and the one intended by this document's
scope (Section 1.3).

## 8. The estimator

The bias sections above assume $\{c_{jn}\}$ (equivalently $\Delta_n$) is known; recovering it
from data is the practical half of this document. This section derives the $J$-dimensional
per-frame estimator, its reduction to the $M=1$ tilt case, and the conditioning and gauge issues
that only appear once $J>2$.

### 8.1 Per-frame normal equations

Run the ordinary piston-model AIA solve to get a current estimate of $(a,u,v,\delta_n)$, and
form the pixel-step residual $r_n(x,y) \equiv I_n(x,y) - \big(a+c_nu+s_nv\big)$. By Eq. (T7),
to first order $r_n \approx \Delta I_n^{(1)} = -w_n\Delta_n$ with $w_n=u\,s_n-v\,c_n$ known (it
depends only on the current $(u,v,\delta_n,g_n)$ estimate, not on the unknown $\Delta_n$).
Substituting $\Delta_n=\sum_j c_{jn}p_j$ and minimizing $\sum_{x,y}\big(r_n+w_n\Delta_n\big)^2$
over $c_{1n},\dots,c_{Jn}$ — an ordinary weighted linear regression across pixels, independent
frame by frame, exactly as `aia_pixel_step` (`aia.md` §"Pixel step") is an independent regression
across frames for each pixel — gives the per-frame $J\times J$ normal equations

$$G^{(n)}\,c_{\cdot n} = -h^{(n)}, \qquad
G^{(n)}_{jj'} \equiv \sum_{x,y} w_n^2\,p_j\,p_{j'}, \qquad
h^{(n)}_j \equiv \sum_{x,y} w_n\,r_n\,p_j \tag{E1}$$

one independent $J\times J$ solve per frame, at fixed $(a,u,v,\delta_n)$ — the direct
$J$-dimensional generalization of the pixel-step idea, run in the other direction (across pixels,
at fixed frame, rather than across frames at fixed pixel).

### 8.2 Reduction to the tilt case

At $M=1$ ($J=2$, $p_1=x,p_2=y$), Eq. (E1) is

$$\begin{pmatrix}\sum w_n^2x^2 & \sum w_n^2xy\\ \sum w_n^2xy & \sum w_n^2y^2\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}k_{xn}\\k_{yn}\end{pmatrix} = -\begin{pmatrix}\sum w_nr_nx\\\sum w_nr_ny\end{pmatrix} \tag{E2}$$

exactly `fit_tilt`'s $2\times2$ system (`phase/methods/residual_tilt.py`), with $w_n$ there named
`bsin` and $-h^{(n)}$ assembled as `-(z @ x), -(z @ y)`. No new content is needed for the tilt
case; Eq. (E1) is its literal generalization.

### 8.3 Conditioning

$G^{(n)}$ is a **$w_n^2$-weighted** Gram matrix of the basis functions, not their plain
(unweighted) Gram matrix — an orthonormal basis under the unweighted field inner product is not
automatically orthogonal under this weight. $w_n(x,y) = u(x,y)s_n-v(x,y)c_n$ vanishes wherever
$\Phi(x,y)+\delta_n$ crosses a node of $\sin(\cdot)$ — frame $n$'s fringe nulls — so $G^{(n)}$ is
built from a weight that is exactly zero along those stripes; a basis term whose shape happens
to concentrate there contributes little to $G^{(n)}$ regardless of how well-conditioned it is in
the plain field inner product. This is a genuinely new failure mode past $M=1$: two basis
directions can be well separated in the plain sense yet become nearly degenerate under a
particular frame's $w_n^2$ weighting. Define, as the per-frame analogue of `aia.md`'s $\kappa_p$,

$$\kappa_\Delta^{(n)} \equiv \operatorname{cond}\big(G^{(n)}\big) \tag{E3}$$

A large $\kappa_\Delta^{(n)}$ for some frame flags that frame's fit to $c_{\cdot n}$ as unreliable
even if the overall AIA solve reports low $\kappa_p,\kappa_{ps}$ — it is a separate, per-pixel-
regression conditioning question, not the temporal one those two diagnostics already cover.
$\max_n\kappa_\Delta^{(n)}$ is a reasonable single summary; a sudden jump when $M$ is increased by
one degree is a sign the added basis term is not well resolved by the fringe pattern actually
recorded, and a candidate reason to stop increasing $M$ regardless of what
`fit_tilt_quality`-style residual metrics say.

### 8.4 Gauge fixing

Eq. (E1) is solved independently per frame and has no mechanism to enforce the frame-mean-zero
gauge, Eq. (T3b) — nothing in a single frame's regression can know what the other frames'
coefficients are. The fitted $\{c_{jn}\}$ will therefore generically carry a nonzero mean
$\bar c_j \equiv \langle c_{jn}\rangle_n$ for each $j$, and by the exact invariance noted at
Eq. (T3b), that mean component is indistinguishable from (and should be treated as) part of
$\Phi$, not part of the step field: project it out before using $\Delta_n$ to correct the data,

$$c_{jn} \;\leftarrow\; c_{jn} - \bar c_j, \qquad j=1,\dots,J \tag{E4}$$

and let the removed static part $\sum_j\bar c_j\,p_j(x,y)$ be picked up on the next pixel/frame-
step re-solve, the way any other static, frame-independent addition to the phase is. Skipping
this step does not change $I_n$ (Eq. T3b is an exact invariance of the forward model) but leaves
an ambiguity in how a real piece of $\Phi$'s shape is reported — split unpredictably between the
recovered $\Phi$ map and the $\{c_{jn}\}$ diagnostics — and, in an iterative refinement loop that
alternates re-fitting $(a,u,v)$ against a step-field-corrected stack, can leave that split
drifting between iterations instead of settling. The failure mode is easiest to see once $J>2$,
but Eq. (T3b) shows it is present already at $M=1$.

## 9. Harmonic fingerprint and vanishing conditions

### 9.1 Discrete-Fourier framing

For the uniform step set $\delta_n = 2\pi n/N$ that minimizes $\kappa_{ps}$ (`aia.md`'s
recommended default), $R_\Delta$, $R_\Delta^{(2)}$, and $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$ are,
respectively, the bin-1, bin-2, and bin-0 discrete Fourier components (in $n$) of the per-frame
step-field sequences $g_n\Delta_n$, $g_n^2\Delta_n$: bin 0 feeds the $\Phi$-independent ramp in
$\Delta\Phi$, bin 1 the $\Delta a$ ripple, bin 2 the $\Delta b$ and oscillatory-$\Delta\Phi$
ripple. This holds for every basis term $j$ independently, since each $\rho_j,\rho_j^{(2)},\mu_j$
of Eq. (T8a) is itself a bin-1/2/0 DFT coefficient of $g_nc_{jn}$, $g_n^2c_{jn}$.

### 9.2 Each bias vanishes iff its own moment does

By Eqs. (T17)–(T19): $\Delta a\equiv0 \iff R_\Delta=0$; the oscillatory part of $\Delta b$ and
$\Delta\Phi$ vanish together iff $R_\Delta^{(2)}=0$; the ramp in $\Delta\Phi$ vanishes iff
$\langle g^2\Delta\rangle=0$. Since each of these is a fixed combination of the per-order moments
$\rho_j,\rho_j^{(2)},\mu_j$ (Eq. T8a), the general vanishing condition is $\rho_j=0$ (resp.
$\rho_j^{(2)}=0$, $\mu_j=0$) for every $j=1,\dots,J$ — no single symmetry nulls all three at once
in general, and adding basis terms (raising $M$) only adds more moments that must each vanish for
the corresponding bias to disappear.

### 9.3 A frame-independent step field is harmless

If the step-field coefficients do not vary from frame to frame, $c_{jn}\equiv c_j$ for every $j$
(only the piston $\delta_n$ steps; the actuator's parasitic field error itself does not drift),
Eq. (T3b)'s gauge is violated maximally ($\bar c_j = c_j$) and by the invariance noted there this
is exactly $\Delta_n(x,y)\equiv\Delta(x,y)=\sum_jc_jp_j(x,y)$ factoring out of every moment in
Eq. (T8):

$$R_\Delta = \Delta(x,y)\,R, \qquad R_\Delta^{(2)} = \Delta(x,y)\,R^{(2)}, \qquad \langle g^2\Delta\rangle = \Delta(x,y)\,\langle g^2\rangle$$

Under the same good-coverage condition of Eq. (T13), `frame_moments.md` §2.4 gives $R=R^{(2)}=0$
exactly, so $R_\Delta=R_\Delta^{(2)}=0$ as well: **a static step-field error, of any order, is
invisible to this mechanism** — it is exactly $\phi_{\text{carrier}}$ picking up an extra
low-order term (whether from a genuine optical carrier or the actuator's own fixed aberration is
not distinguishable, and does not need to be, since both are removed together by carrier
fitting), and only the surviving $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$ term feeds a proportional addition
into $\Delta\Phi$'s already-harmless ramp. It is specifically a step field that **changes from
frame to frame** — as in the worked case below — that produces the $\Delta a$, $\Delta b$
fringes of Sections 5–6. At $M=1$ this is exactly "a frame-independent tilt is invisible"; the
underlying reason (the gauge invariance of Eq. T3b) applies identically at any $M$.

### 9.4 Worked case: a monotone drift

Take $g_n\equiv1$, the uniform step set $\delta_n=2\pi n/N$ ($n=0,\dots,N-1$), and a step field
that drifts linearly over the sweep, $c_{jn}=\kappa_j n$ for constants $\kappa_j$ — e.g. a
stepping actuator whose parasitic aberration accumulates with each step rather than resetting.
This is unrestricted in $M$: $p(x,y)$ can be the tilt basis, a quadratic basis, or any mix.
Then $\Delta_n(x,y) = n\Lambda(x,y)$ with $\Lambda(x,y) \equiv \sum_j\kappa_jp_j(x,y)$ any fixed,
zero-field-mean spatial pattern. With $\omega\equiv e^{i2\pi/N}$,

$$R_\Delta = \Lambda\Big\langle n\,\omega^n\Big\rangle_n = \frac{\Lambda}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}n\,\omega^n = \frac{\Lambda}{\omega-1}$$

using the standard identity $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}n\,\omega^n = N/(\omega-1)$ for $\omega^N=1,\
\omega\ne1$ (verified by differentiating $\sum_n\omega^n=(\omega^N-1)/(\omega-1)=0$). Since
$|\omega-1| = 2\sin(\pi/N)$,

$$|R_\Delta| = \frac{|\Lambda(x,y)|}{2\sin(\pi/N)} \tag{T23}$$

The same identity with $\omega\to\omega^2$ (also an $N$-th root of unity, distinct from $1$ for
$N\ge3$) gives $R_\Delta^{(2)} = \Lambda/(\omega^2-1)$, so

$$\big|R_\Delta^{(2)}\big| = \frac{|\Lambda(x,y)|}{2\sin(2\pi/N)} \tag{T24}$$

Both amplitudes are set by $|\Lambda(x,y)|$ — the *total* excursion accumulated between the
first and last frame at that pixel, in whatever spatial pattern the actuator's drift happens to
have — not by the number of frames averaging it down: for large $N$, $\sin(\pi/N)\approx\pi/N$
so $|R_\Delta| \to N|\Lambda|/2\pi = (\text{total excursion})/2\pi$, i.e. the bias saturates to a
fixed fraction of the drift's total span rather than shrinking. This is the opposite of the
$1/\sqrt N$ contrast-jitter floor found for uncorrelated $g_n$ in `frame_moments.md` §7.3 — **a
systematic, monotone step-field drift does not average away by acquiring more frames**, whatever
its spatial order, mirroring `frame_moments.md`'s conclusion for a $\delta$–$g$ correlated
jitter (§7.4).

## 11. Assumptions used

1. **Origin at the field centroid, each basis term zero-mean, and coefficients zero-frame-mean**
   (§1.2) — the first makes the piston/step-field split of Eq. (T1) exact ($\delta_n$ is the
   spatial mean of the true step, $\Delta_n$ its zero-mean remainder); the second
   (Eq. T3b) is an *exact* gauge fixing an otherwise real ambiguity between $\Delta_n$ and
   $\Phi$, needed to define the per-frame estimator of §8 uniquely once $J>1$.
2. **Small step-field error, expansion to second order** (§2.2) — $\Delta_n$ is treated as small
   enough that $O(\Delta_n^3)$ terms are negligible; this is the "small error" regime the
   original request specified, not a claim that any size of error is acceptable.
3. **Piston $\delta_n$ held at its true value; only the pixel step is analyzed** (§1.3, §7) — the
   frame step's own sensitivity to $\Delta_n$ is argued, not derived, to be a smaller, higher-
   order effect under good coverage that degrades somewhat as $M$ grows; a full self-consistent
   treatment is outside this document's scope.
4. **Good $\delta$/$g$ coverage for the diagonalization of $A_p$** (§4.3) — uniform steps and
   constant $g_n$, giving $R=R^{(2)}=0$ (`frame_moments.md` §2.4, $N\ge3$). Without it, $A_p$'s
   off-diagonal terms (Eq. T12) mix into $\Delta a,\Delta b,\Delta\Phi$ as an additional,
   unanalyzed source of bias, compounding a large $\kappa_p$.
5. **No noise** (§4.1) — the propagation isolates the deterministic step-field bias; measurement
   noise adds its own, independent term to Eq. (T10) and is not carried through this document.
6. **Truncation at degree $M$** (§1.1, §8) — the un-modeled degree-$>M$ remainder of the true
   step field biases the degree-$M$ fit exactly the way the full $\Delta_n$ biased the piston-
   only model, one level down; §5.6/§8.3's diagnostics are the practical check for whether that
   remainder still matters at the current $M$.
