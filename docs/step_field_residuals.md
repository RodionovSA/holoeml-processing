# Spatially-varying phase-step residuals in AIA

This document derives, in closed form, the bias that a **spatially-varying phase-step error**
puts into the fields recovered by the piston-model AIA solve of [`aia.md`](aia.md), and derives
the algorithm that recovers and corrects for it. `interference_model.md` Eq. (9a)/(9b) already
carries this step field, $\Delta(x,y,t)$, in the full model; `aia.md` solves that model's
**uniform-piston limit** (its Eq. 20, $\Delta_n\equiv0$). This document quantifies what that
limit costs when $\Delta_n$ is not in fact negligible, and derives the estimator
(`phase.methods.step_field`) that recovers $\Delta_n$ from the AIA residual instead of assuming
it away.

The derivation is pointwise in $(x,y)$ and holds for *any* per-frame field, so the pure linear
tilt $\Delta_n(x,y) = k_{xn}x+k_{yn}y$ is treated throughout as one special case ($M=1$) of the
general truncated-Taylor step field, not as a separate model.

As in `interference_model.md` and `aia.md`, the $(x,y)$ dependence of $a$, $b$, $\Phi$ (and, here,
of the step-field coefficients) is suppressed for readability; every equation holds pixel-by-pixel
unless stated otherwise.

## 1. Setup

### 1.1 The general step field

`interference_model.md` Eq. (9a)/(9b) splits the true phase step into a piston $\delta_n$ and a
zero-mean spatial remainder $\Delta_n(x,y)$, expanded on a degree-$M$ polynomial basis. Restated
per frame:

$$\delta_n(x,y) = \delta_n + \Delta_n(x,y), \qquad
\Delta_n(x,y) = \sum_{j=1}^{J} c_{jn}\,p_j(x,y) \tag{T1}$$

where $p_1,\dots,p_J$ are the field's monomials of total degree $1$ through $M$ ($x$, $y$, $x^2$,
$xy$, $y^2$, …, so $J=\tfrac{(M+1)(M+2)}{2}-1$), and $c_{jn}$ is frame $n$'s coefficient of basis
term $j$. Degree $0$ is excluded from the basis — that is exactly the piston $\delta_n$. $M=1$
(so $p_1=x,\ p_2=y$, $c_{1n}=k_{xn}$, $c_{2n}=k_{yn}$) is the pure linear tilt; $M=0$ gives $J=0$
— no basis terms, $\Delta_n(x,y)\equiv0$ — `aia.md`'s uniform-piston limit.

The true per-frame intensity, `interference_model.md` Eq. (17) with $\alpha_n$ already divided
out (`aia.md` §"Starting point"), is

$$I_n(x,y) = a(x,y) + g_n\,b(x,y)\cos\big(\Phi(x,y) + \delta_n + \Delta_n(x,y)\big) \tag{T2}$$

`aia.md`'s pixel step (its §"Pixel step") is fit to data generated by Eq. (T2) while its design
matrix $A$ (`aia.md` Eq. 5) is built from the piston $\delta_n$ alone — $\Delta_n$ does not
appear in the model the solver assumes. This document computes the resulting bias in the
recovered $(a, u, v)$ and, from them, $(a, b, \Phi)$, then (§8) derives an estimator that
recovers $\{c_{jn}\}$ from the AIA residual.

Everywhere below, $g_n$ is treated as a known value at the point the linearization is taken
around — this derivation does not care whether it came from a separate measurement or from
`aia.md`'s joint fit. `phase.methods.step_field.aia_step_field` re-derives $g_n$ (and the AIA
pixel/frame step it re-runs each refinement round) from the current step-field-corrected data
when gain is jointly fit, so $g_n$ can change between refinement rounds; each round's
$\{c_{jn}\}$ estimate (§8) is still computed against that round's own $(a,u,v,g)$, exactly as
this document assumes.

### 1.2 Origin and gauge conventions

Two conventions make Eq. (T1)'s decomposition unambiguous — one spatial, one temporal. Both are
already stated in `interference_model.md`; restated here in the notation this document uses.

**(i) Origin at the field centroid, each $p_j$ zero-mean over the field** (`interference_model.md`
Eq. 9a). Place the origin of $(x,y)$ at the field centroid and choose each basis function to have
zero spatial average,

$$\big\langle p_j(x,y)\big\rangle_{x,y} = 0, \qquad j=1,\dots,J \tag{T3}$$

so that $\big\langle \Delta_n(x,y)\big\rangle_{x,y} = 0$ for every $n$ — this is what makes
$\delta_n$ well-defined as *the* spatial mean of the true step. For $M=1$, $p_1=x,\ p_2=y$
already satisfy Eq. (T3) once the origin sits at the centroid; for $M\ge2$ each higher-order
monomial must be mean-subtracted explicitly (e.g. $p=x^2-\langle x^2\rangle_{x,y}$). This is a
constraint on the field average, not on the value at the centroid — $x^2-\langle
x^2\rangle_{x,y}$ is generally nonzero at $x=0$; §5.6 returns to why that distinction matters.

**(ii) Frame-mean-zero coefficients** (`interference_model.md` Eq. 9b):

$$\big\langle c_{jn}\big\rangle_n = 0, \qquad j=1,\dots,J \tag{T3b}$$

This tracks an *exact* invariance of Eq. (T2): shifting every $c_{jn} \to c_{jn}-\bar c_j$ while
adding $\bar c_j\,p_j(x,y)$ to $\Phi(x,y)$ leaves $I_n$ unchanged for every $n$ and pixel — the
static part of a step-field coefficient is indistinguishable from a static addition to the phase
(a piece of $\phi_{\text{carrier}}$, `interference_model.md` Eq. 15). Eq. (T3b) is the spatial
analogue of `aia.md`'s phase-origin pin $\delta_1=0$ for the piston, and §8.4 shows it is needed
to define the estimator uniquely for any $M\ge1$, since a per-frame least-squares fit (§8.1) has
no way to enforce it on its own.

### 1.3 Scope: $\delta_n$ is not the unknown here

$\delta_n$ itself is not assumed wrong here — only the field error $\Delta_n$ is unmodeled.
Concretely: this derivation propagates $\Delta_n$'s effect through the **pixel step** (`aia.md`
§"Pixel step") at a fixed, correct piston $\delta_n$ — the same linear solve that step already
performs, unmodified. The companion question — whether $\Delta_n$ also biases the **frame
step**'s own estimate $\hat\delta_n$ (`aia.md` Eq. 6–8), so that the piston fed into the pixel
step is itself already off — is addressed separately in §7, and shown there to be a strictly
smaller, second-order effect under the same good-coverage condition `aia.md` already invokes for
$\kappa_{ps}$, though one that degrades as $M$ grows. Sections 2–6 hold $\delta_n$ fixed at its
true value.

## 2. Linearization of the perturbed model

Nothing in this section uses the spatial shape of $\Delta_n$ — it holds pointwise at every
$(x,y)$ for whatever value $\Delta_n$ takes there. The polynomial structure only enters once
these per-pixel results are combined across pixels (§3 onward).

### 2.1 Quadrature shorthand for the design-matrix rows

`aia.md` Eq. (5) builds $A$ from rows $[1,\ g_n\cos\delta_n,\ g_n\sin\delta_n]$. Write

$$P_n \equiv g_n\cos\delta_n, \qquad Q_n \equiv g_n\sin\delta_n \tag{T4}$$

so the noise-free piston prediction (`aia.md` Eq. 3) is $I_n^{(0)} = a + P_n u + Q_n v$, with $u,
v$ the quadrature fields of `aia.md` Eq. (2).

### 2.2 Expanding the cosine in $\Delta_n$

Expand Eq. (T2)'s cosine using $\cos(\theta+\Delta) = \cos\theta\cos\Delta - \sin\theta\sin\Delta$
with $\theta = \Phi+\delta_n$, then Taylor-expand $\cos\Delta_n \approx 1 - \tfrac12\Delta_n^2$
and $\sin\Delta_n \approx \Delta_n$ (dropping $O(\Delta_n^3)$, consistent with keeping terms
through second order):

$$\cos(\Phi+\delta_n+\Delta_n) \approx \cos(\Phi+\delta_n) - \Delta_n\sin(\Phi+\delta_n) - \tfrac12\Delta_n^2\cos(\Phi+\delta_n) \tag{T5}$$

Substituting into Eq. (T2) and collecting orders in $\Delta_n$, $I_n = I_n^{(0)} + \Delta I_n^{(1)} + \Delta I_n^{(2)} + O(\Delta_n^3)$ with

$$\Delta I_n^{(1)} = -g_n b\,\Delta_n\sin(\Phi+\delta_n), \qquad
\Delta I_n^{(2)} = -\tfrac12 g_n b\,\Delta_n^2\cos(\Phi+\delta_n) \tag{T6}$$

### 2.3 Converting to quadrature variables

Expand $\sin(\Phi+\delta_n) = \sin\Phi\cos\delta_n+\cos\Phi\sin\delta_n$ and use
$b\cos\Phi = u$, $b\sin\Phi = -v$:

$$g_nb\sin(\Phi+\delta_n) = g_n\big[(-v)\cos\delta_n + u\sin\delta_n\big] = u\,Q_n - v\,P_n$$

and, by the same steps with $\cos\Phi,\sin\Phi$ in place of $\sin\Phi,\cos\Phi$,
$g_nb\cos(\Phi+\delta_n) = u\,P_n+v\,Q_n$, so

$$\Delta I_n^{(1)} = \Delta_n\,(v\,P_n - u\,Q_n), \qquad
\Delta I_n^{(2)} = -\tfrac12\Delta_n^2\,(u\,P_n + v\,Q_n) \tag{T7}$$

Define $w_n \equiv u\,Q_n - v\,P_n = -(v\,P_n-u\,Q_n)$, so Eq. (T7) reads
$\Delta I_n^{(1)} = -w_n\,\Delta_n$; $w_n = \partial I_n^{(0)}/\partial(-\delta_n)$ is exactly the
sensitivity of the noise-free piston prediction to a step-timing shift at frame $n$. $w_n$
reappears as the regressor of the estimator in §8; its derivative-of-a-cosine-is-a-sine origin is
the reason every bias below comes out in quadrature ($\sin$, not $\cos$) with the corresponding
piston-timing quantities of §4.3.

## 3. Step-field-weighted frame moments

Define, weighted by the step field $\Delta_n$ (a field-dependent, zero-frame-mean quantity —
Eq. T3b — not a constant, so these residuals are themselves functions of $(x,y)$):

$$R_\Delta = \big\langle g_n\Delta_n\,e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n, \qquad
\langle g^2\Delta\rangle = \big\langle g_n^2\Delta_n\big\rangle_n, \qquad
R_\Delta^{(2)} = \big\langle g_n^2\Delta_n\,e^{i2\delta_n}\big\rangle_n \tag{T8}$$

with $\psi_\Delta = \arg R_\Delta$, $\psi_{\Delta2} = \arg R_\Delta^{(2)}$, and real/imaginary
parts $R_{\Delta} = R_{\Delta,c}+iR_{\Delta,s}$, $R_\Delta^{(2)} = R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}+iR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}$.
Setting $\Delta_n\to1$ in Eq. (T8) gives the corresponding **unweighted** frame moments used from
§4.3 on:

$$R = \big\langle g_n\,e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n = R_c+iR_s, \qquad
\langle g^2\rangle = \big\langle g_n^2\big\rangle_n, \qquad
R^{(2)} = \big\langle g_n^2\,e^{i2\delta_n}\big\rangle_n = R_c^{(2)}+iR_s^{(2)} \tag{T8'}$$

Since $\Delta_n$ is itself linear in the coefficients $c_{jn}$ (Eq. T1) and frame-averaging is
linear, each field in Eq. (T8) decomposes exactly into the fixed spatial basis:

$$R_\Delta(x,y) = \sum_{j=1}^J \rho_j\,p_j(x,y), \quad \rho_j \equiv \big\langle g_n c_{jn}e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n; \qquad
\langle g^2\Delta\rangle(x,y) = \sum_{j=1}^J \mu_j\,p_j(x,y), \quad \mu_j \equiv \big\langle g_n^2 c_{jn}\big\rangle_n \tag{T8a}$$

and likewise $R_\Delta^{(2)}(x,y) = \sum_j \rho_j^{(2)}p_j(x,y)$ with
$\rho_j^{(2)} = \langle g_n^2 c_{jn}e^{i2\delta_n}\rangle_n$ — one complex (or, for
$\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$, real) number per basis term, computable directly from the realized
$\{c_{jn}\}$ and $\{\delta_n,g_n\}$ sequences.

Section 6 needs the second-order-in-$\Delta_n$ counterparts. Since $\Delta_n^2 = \sum_{j,j'}
c_{jn}c_{j'n}\,p_jp_{j'}$ is quadratic in the coefficients, these decompose into the basis's
pairwise products instead:

$$S(x,y) = \big\langle g_n\Delta_n^2\,e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n = \sum_{j,j'}\sigma_{jj'}\,p_jp_{j'}, \quad
\sigma_{jj'} \equiv \big\langle g_n c_{jn}c_{j'n}e^{i\delta_n}\big\rangle_n \tag{T9}$$

and likewise $S^{(2)}(x,y)=\sum_{jj'}\sigma_{jj'}^{(2)}p_jp_{j'}$,
$S^{(0)}(x,y)=\sum_{jj'}\sigma_{jj'}^{(0)}p_jp_{j'}$ with
$\sigma_{jj'}^{(0)}=\langle g_n^2c_{jn}c_{j'n}\rangle_n$. $\Sigma^{(0)}\equiv[\sigma_{jj'}^{(0)}]$
is the frame-average of the rank-1 matrices $g_n^2\,c_{\cdot n}c_{\cdot n}^\top$, each positive
semi-definite, so $\Sigma^{(0)}$ itself is PSD; consequently $S^{(0)}(x,y) = p(x,y)^\top
\Sigma^{(0)}p(x,y) \ge 0$ at every pixel. With $\psi_S=\arg S$, $\psi_{S2}=\arg S^{(2)}$ as before.

## 4. Propagating the perturbation through the pixel step

### 4.1 The pixel step as an exact linear solve

`aia.md` Eq. (5) gives the design matrix $A$ (rows $[1,P_n,Q_n]$); the pixel step solves
$(a,u,v)^\top = A_p^{-1}A^\top I$ with $A_p \equiv A^\top A$ (`aia.md`'s pixel-step normal
matrix). Because $I_n^{(0)} = a+P_nu+Q_nv$ lies exactly in the column space of $A$ by
construction, $A_p^{-1}A^\top I^{(0)} = (a,u,v)^\top$ exactly, so for $I = I^{(0)}+\Delta I$ the
solve returns

$$(\hat a,\hat u,\hat v)^\top = (a,u,v)^\top + A_p^{-1}A^\top\Delta I
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(\Delta a,\Delta u,\Delta v)^\top \equiv A_p^{-1}A^\top\Delta I \tag{T10}$$

by linearity of least squares. (Measurement noise adds its own, statistically independent term
to Eq. (T10); it is not carried here, since this document isolates the deterministic bias.) This
step is pointwise in $(x,y)$ and never uses the spatial shape of $\Delta_n$.

### 4.2 Componentwise right-hand side

Using $\Delta I_n^{(1)}$ from Eq. (T7), $A^\top\Delta I^{(1)} = N\big(\langle\Delta
I_n^{(1)}\rangle_n,\ \langle P_n\Delta I_n^{(1)}\rangle_n,\ \langle Q_n\Delta
I_n^{(1)}\rangle_n\big)^\top$. The first entry is immediate:

$$\big\langle\Delta I_n^{(1)}\big\rangle_n = v\langle P_n\Delta_n\rangle_n - u\langle Q_n\Delta_n\rangle_n = vR_{\Delta,c} - uR_{\Delta,s}$$

The other two need $\langle P_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n$, $\langle Q_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n$,
$\langle P_nQ_n\Delta_n\rangle_n$. Using the standard double-angle identities
$P_n^2=\tfrac12g_n^2(1+\cos2\delta_n)$, $Q_n^2=\tfrac12g_n^2(1-\cos2\delta_n)$,
$P_nQ_n=\tfrac12g_n^2\sin2\delta_n$:

$$\langle P_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n = \tfrac12\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + \tfrac12R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}, \qquad
\langle Q_n^2\Delta_n\rangle_n = \tfrac12\langle g^2\Delta\rangle - \tfrac12R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}, \qquad
\langle P_nQ_n\Delta_n\rangle_n = \tfrac12R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}$$

so

$$\big\langle P_n\Delta I_n^{(1)}\big\rangle_n = \tfrac12v\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + \tfrac12\big(vR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}-uR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}\big), \qquad
\big\langle Q_n\Delta I_n^{(1)}\big\rangle_n = -\tfrac12u\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + \tfrac12\big(vR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}+uR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}\big) \tag{T11}$$

### 4.3 The pixel-step normal matrix in these moments

The same double-angle identities give $A_p/N$ in the unweighted moments of Eq. (T8'), by the
identical computation with $\Delta_n\to1$:

$$\frac{A_p}{N} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & R_c & R_s \\ R_c & \tfrac12(\langle g^2\rangle+R_c^{(2)}) & \tfrac12R_s^{(2)} \\ R_s & \tfrac12R_s^{(2)} & \tfrac12(\langle g^2\rangle-R_c^{(2)}) \end{pmatrix} \tag{T12}$$

Inverting Eq. (T12) in general is possible but not illuminating; the acquisitions this package
targets are run at the good-coverage condition `aia.md` already recommends for a low $\kappa_p$
— uniform steps $\delta_n=2\pi n/N$ and constant $g_n$, for which $R=R^{(2)}=0$ *exactly* for
$N\ge3$ (both are sums, over $n$, of $N$-th roots of unity and their squares). Eq. (T12) then
diagonalizes,

$$\frac{A_p}{N} = \operatorname{diag}\!\Big(1,\ \tfrac12\langle g^2\rangle,\ \tfrac12\langle g^2\rangle\Big)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_p^{-1} = \frac1N\operatorname{diag}\!\Big(1,\ \tfrac{2}{\langle g^2\rangle},\ \tfrac{2}{\langle g^2\rangle}\Big) \tag{T13}$$

and this is the $A_p^{-1}$ used from here on. (A poorly-conditioned, non-uniform step set biases
$\Delta a,\Delta u,\Delta v$ through the off-diagonal terms of Eq. (T12) as well as through the
step-field moments of Eq. (T8) — a second, compounding source of the large-$\kappa_p$
inaccuracy `aia.md`'s diagnostics already warn about, not analyzed further here.)

Combining Eqs. (T10), (T11), (T13):

$$\Delta a = vR_{\Delta,c} - uR_{\Delta,s}, \qquad
\Delta u = \frac{v\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + vR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}-uR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}}{\langle g^2\rangle}, \qquad
\Delta v = \frac{-u\langle g^2\Delta\rangle + vR_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}+uR_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}}{\langle g^2\rangle} \tag{T14}$$

## 5. First-order bias in $a$, $b$, $\Phi$

### 5.1 A quadrature lemma

$b=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$, $\Phi=\operatorname{atan2}(-v,u)$ (`aia.md` Eq. 9) give, by direct
differentiation,

$$\Delta b = \cos\Phi\,\Delta u - \sin\Phi\,\Delta v, \qquad
\Delta\Phi = \frac{-\sin\Phi\,\Delta u - \cos\Phi\,\Delta v}{b} \tag{T15}$$

valid to first order in $(\Delta u,\Delta v)$. Substituting $u=b\cos\Phi,\ v=-b\sin\Phi$ gives
four identities used repeatedly below and in §6:

$$u\cos\Phi - v\sin\Phi = b, \qquad u\cos\Phi + v\sin\Phi = b\cos2\Phi, \qquad
v\cos\Phi + u\sin\Phi = 0, \qquad v\cos\Phi - u\sin\Phi = -b\sin2\Phi \tag{T16}$$

### 5.2 $\Delta a$ — first harmonic

From Eq. (T14), $\Delta a = vR_{\Delta,c}-uR_{\Delta,s}$. Writing $R_{\Delta,c}=|R_\Delta|\cos\psi_\Delta$,
$R_{\Delta,s}=|R_\Delta|\sin\psi_\Delta$ and substituting $u=b\cos\Phi,\ v=-b\sin\Phi$:

$$\Delta a = -b|R_\Delta|(\sin\Phi\cos\psi_\Delta+\cos\Phi\sin\psi_\Delta) = -b\,|R_\Delta|\sin(\Phi+\psi_\Delta) = -b\,\operatorname{Im}\!\big[R_\Delta e^{i\Phi}\big] \tag{T17}$$

### 5.3 $\Delta b$ — second harmonic

Substitute $\Delta u,\Delta v$ (Eq. T14) into $\Delta b=\cos\Phi\,\Delta u-\sin\Phi\,\Delta v$:

$$\langle g^2\rangle\,\Delta b = \langle g^2\Delta\rangle\,(v\cos\Phi+u\sin\Phi)
+ R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}(v\cos\Phi-u\sin\Phi) - R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}(u\cos\Phi+v\sin\Phi)$$

By Eq. (T16) the first bracket is $0$ — **the step-field-weighted carrier moment $\langle
g^2\Delta\rangle$ drops out of $\Delta b$ entirely** — and the remaining two collapse via
Eq. (T16) to $-b\big[R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}\sin2\Phi+R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}\cos2\Phi\big] =
-b|R_\Delta^{(2)}|\sin(2\Phi+\psi_{\Delta2})$, so

$$\Delta b = -\frac{b\,|R_\Delta^{(2)}|}{\langle g^2\rangle}\sin(2\Phi+\psi_{\Delta2}) = -\frac{b}{\langle g^2\rangle}\operatorname{Im}\!\big[R_\Delta^{(2)}e^{i2\Phi}\big] \tag{T18}$$

### 5.4 $\Delta\Phi$ — a ramp plus a second harmonic

Substitute the same $\Delta u,\Delta v$ into $\Delta\Phi = (-\sin\Phi\,\Delta u-\cos\Phi\,\Delta v)/b$
and collect by $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$, $R_{\Delta,c}^{(2)}$, $R_{\Delta,s}^{(2)}$ exactly as in
§5.3, using Eq. (T16)'s coefficients:

$$\Delta\Phi = \frac{\langle g^2\Delta\rangle}{\langle g^2\rangle} - \frac{|R_\Delta^{(2)}|}{\langle g^2\rangle}\cos(2\Phi+\psi_{\Delta2})
= \frac{\langle g^2\Delta\rangle}{\langle g^2\rangle} - \frac{1}{\langle g^2\rangle}\operatorname{Re}\!\big[R_\Delta^{(2)}e^{i2\Phi}\big] \tag{T19}$$

### 5.5 The harmonic fingerprint

Eqs. (T17)–(T19): **$\Delta a$ oscillates at the first harmonic of $\Phi$ ($|R_\Delta|$), $\Delta
b$ at the second ($|R_\Delta^{(2)}|$)**, both riding $\sin$ ($\operatorname{Im}[\,\cdot\,]$) — a
direct consequence of Eq. (T7)'s $\Delta I_n^{(1)} = -w_n\Delta_n$: a derivative of a cosine is a
sine, so a step-field bias sits in quadrature with a piston-timing bias of the same harmonic
order. $\Delta\Phi$ additionally carries a phase-independent ramp,
$\langle g^2\Delta\rangle/\langle g^2\rangle$ — a spatially-smooth addition to the recovered
phase, of exactly the functional form of $\phi_{\text{carrier}}$ (`interference_model.md`
Eq. 15), and removed the same way (carrier fitting/subtraction); it is not a fringe artifact and
is set aside from here on. None of Eqs. (T17)–(T19) used the spatial shape of $\Delta_n$ —
$|R_\Delta|,|R_\Delta^{(2)}|,\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$ are, per Eq. (T8a), themselves degree-$M$
polynomials in $(x,y)$; how these envelopes vary with position is taken up next.

### 5.6 The spatial envelope of each bias, and a diagnostic

By Eq. (T8a), each envelope $R_\Delta$, $R_\Delta^{(2)}$, $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$ is a fixed
linear combination of the same degree-$1\ldots M$ basis used for $\Delta_n$ itself, so each has
zero field average (every $p_j$ does, Eq. T3) but is not generally zero at the centroid itself
for $M\ge2$ — a curvature term contributes a bowl- or saddle-shaped envelope, zero on average but
often largest in magnitude at the centroid. Only the pure-tilt terms ($j\in\{1,2\}$, $p_1=x,p_2=y$)
vanish exactly at the centroid and grow linearly outward (the worked case of §9.3); a genuine
quadratic-or-higher remainder need not share that property.

Either way, the envelope is always a bounded, low-order (degree $\le M$, so $\le 2M$ for the
second-order terms of §6) polynomial — smooth and slowly varying compared to the fringe pattern
itself. This gives a usable diagnostic: a residual ripple whose RMS amplitude is essentially
**uniform across the field** cannot be produced by any finite-degree $\Delta_n(x,y)$ — its cause
lies elsewhere, most plausibly a phase-locked defect $\varepsilon(\Phi)$ from an imperfect frame
model (`phase/ripple.py`'s `estimate_phase_ripple` targets exactly this). A residual that instead
varies smoothly and systematically with position — larger away from the centroid, or with a
visible bowl/saddle shape — is the signature of an unmodeled step-field remainder, and the one to
look for before increasing $M$.

## 6. Second order, where the first order vanishes

$\Delta b$ (Eq. T18) has no phase-independent term — a step-field error cannot show up as an
apparent loss of fringe visibility at first order. That effect appears only at second order in
$\Delta_n$, via $\Delta I_n^{(2)}$ (Eq. T7). Repeating §4.2's computation with $\Delta I_n^{(2)}$
in place of $\Delta I_n^{(1)}$ (same double-angle identities, now producing the second-order
moments of Eq. T9) gives

$$\Delta u^{(2)} = -\frac{uS^{(0)}+uS_c^{(2)}+vS_s^{(2)}}{2\langle g^2\rangle}, \qquad
\Delta v^{(2)} = -\frac{vS^{(0)}-vS_c^{(2)}+uS_s^{(2)}}{2\langle g^2\rangle} \tag{T20}$$

Substituting into $\Delta b^{(2)} = \cos\Phi\,\Delta u^{(2)}-\sin\Phi\,\Delta v^{(2)}$ and
grouping by $S^{(0)}$, $S_c^{(2)}$, $S_s^{(2)}$ (Eq. T16's coefficients again):

$$\Delta b^{(2)} = -\frac{b}{2\langle g^2\rangle}\Big[S^{(0)} + S_c^{(2)}\cos2\Phi - S_s^{(2)}\sin2\Phi\Big]
= -\frac{b}{2\langle g^2\rangle}\Big[S^{(0)} + |S^{(2)}|\cos(2\Phi+\psi_{S2})\Big] \tag{T21}$$

and, by the same substitution into $\Delta\Phi^{(2)} = (-\sin\Phi\,\Delta u^{(2)}-\cos\Phi\,\Delta v^{(2)})/b$,

$$\Delta\Phi^{(2)} = \frac{S_c^{(2)}\sin2\Phi+S_s^{(2)}\cos2\Phi}{2\langle g^2\rangle} = \frac{|S^{(2)}|}{2\langle g^2\rangle}\sin(2\Phi+\psi_{S2}) \tag{T22}$$

Since $S^{(0)}(x,y)=p(x,y)^\top\Sigma^{(0)}p(x,y)\ge0$ always (§3), the phase-independent part of
Eq. (T21) is **strictly a visibility loss**, $-bS^{(0)}/(2\langle g^2\rangle) \le 0$. For a pure
tilt ($M=1$) $S^{(0)}\propto x^2+xy+y^2$ grows quadratically with distance from the field
centroid; for a general degree-$M$ step field $S^{(0)}$ is a degree-$2M$ polynomial (a quadratic
step field, $M=2$, gives a **quartic** visibility roll-off) — either way it mimics, in shape, a
genuine roll-off of the coherence envelope $\gamma(x,y)$ that `interference_model.md` Eq. (13)
already folds into $b(x,y)$: a step-field-induced $b$ roll-off and a real $\gamma$ roll-off are
not distinguishable from a single acquisition's recovered $b$ map alone.

$\Delta a$'s own second-order term, $\Delta a^{(2)} = -\tfrac12(uS_c+vS_s) =
-\tfrac{b}{2}\operatorname{Re}[Se^{i\Phi}]$, is still purely first-harmonic — it corrects the size
and phase of the same $\Delta a$ ripple already present at first order (Eq. T17), rather than
introducing a new harmonic. Unlike $b$ and $\Phi$, $a$ therefore needs no second-order term to
see a qualitatively new effect.

## 7. Why the frame step does not, to first order, re-absorb the step field

§1.3 deferred whether $\Delta_n$ also biases the frame step's own estimate $\hat\delta_n$
(`aia.md` Eq. 6–8), which would feed a wrong piston back into the pixel step above. The frame
step regresses each frame's $P=H\times W$ pixel values onto $[1,u,v]$ across the field (`aia.md`
Eq. 6–7); the step field's contribution to frame $n$'s pixel values, $\Delta I_n^{(1)}(x,y) =
-w_n(x,y)\,\Delta_n(x,y)$ (Eq. T7), is degree $\le M$ in $(x,y)$, while the regressors $u,v$
carry the field's fringe structure. Under the same good-coverage condition already invoked for
Eq. (T13) — uniform steps, constant $g_n$, $\kappa_{ps}$ near its floor of 2 (`aia.md`
§"Accuracy diagnostics") — $u,v$ are spread as evenly as possible over the unit circle rather
than correlated with any single low-order spatial pattern, so the coupling (the projection of
$\{w_n\,p_j\}_j$ onto $\operatorname{span}\{1,u,v\}$) stays weak for a low-order $p_j$. That
coupling grows with $M$, though: a higher-degree $p_j$ has more freedom to correlate with
whatever smooth, low-order structure $u,v$ already carry (e.g. a large sample-phase gradient, or
$\phi_{\text{carrier}}$ itself) — the reason to keep $M$ small (2–3) rather than push it
arbitrarily high. Quantifying that residual would require the frame step's own normal matrix
$A_{ps}=B^\top B$ and is not worked out here; the pixel-step bias of §4–6, computed at the true
(unshifted) $\delta_n$, is the leading-order effect and the one this document scopes to (§1.3).

## 8. The estimator and the algorithm

The bias sections above assume $\{c_{jn}\}$ (equivalently $\Delta_n$) is known; recovering it
from data, and using it to correct an AIA solve, is the practical half of this document.

### 8.1 Per-frame normal equations

Run the piston-model AIA solve (`aia.md`) to get a current estimate of $(a,u,v,\delta_n,g_n)$
and, if gain is jointly fit, the per-frame offset $c_n$ of `aia.md` Eq. (4) — the frame $I_n$
below is understood net of that offset, exactly as `fit_step_field` receives it. Form the
pixel-step residual $r_n(x,y) \equiv I_n(x,y) - \big(a+P_nu+Q_nv\big)$. By Eq. (T7), to first
order $r_n \approx \Delta I_n^{(1)} = -w_n\Delta_n$ with $w_n=uQ_n-vP_n$ known (it depends only on
the current $(u,v,\delta_n,g_n)$ estimate, not on the unknown $\Delta_n$). Substituting
$\Delta_n=\sum_j c_{jn}p_j$ and minimizing $\sum_{x,y}\big(r_n+w_n\Delta_n\big)^2$ over
$c_{1n},\dots,c_{Jn}$ — an ordinary weighted linear regression across pixels, independent frame
by frame — gives the per-frame $J\times J$ normal equations

$$G^{(n)}\,c_{\cdot n} = -h^{(n)}, \qquad
G^{(n)}_{jj'} \equiv \sum_{x,y} w_n^2\,p_j\,p_{j'}, \qquad
h^{(n)}_j \equiv \sum_{x,y} w_n\,r_n\,p_j \tag{E1}$$

one independent $J\times J$ solve per frame, at fixed $(a,u,v,\delta_n)$ — the direct
$J$-dimensional generalization of the pixel-step idea, run in the other direction (across pixels,
at fixed frame, rather than across frames at fixed pixel). At $M=1$ ($J=2$, $p_1=x,p_2=y$),
Eq. (E1) is simply the $2\times2$ system for the frame's tilt coefficients $(k_{xn},k_{yn})$.

**Why first order is enough here, despite §6.** Eq. (E1) uses only $\Delta I_n^{(1)}$: it is
linear in $\Delta_n$ (hence in $c_{jn}$), which is what makes it a closed-form linear regression
in the first place. $\Delta I_n^{(2)}$ (§6) is quadratic in $c_{jn}$ and would turn Eq. (E1) into
a nonlinear per-frame fit with no closed form — the second-order derivation exists to bound the
bias left by *not* correcting $\Delta_n$ at all (§5–6 assume the plain, uncorrected AIA solve),
not to be folded into the correction itself. The algorithm below instead recovers that missing
accuracy by iterating: each round fits the first-order model, corrects the data, and re-solves,
so the residual shrinks by roughly $\Delta_n$'s own size each round and the next round's
first-order fit absorbs what would otherwise have been the second-order term — a Gauss-Newton-style
refinement rather than a one-shot second-order fit. This also sidesteps a second-order term that
is a poor fit target on its own: its leading effect is a phase-independent visibility loss in $b$
(Eq. T21) indistinguishable from a genuine coherence-envelope roll-off $\gamma(x,y)$ (§6, end), so
even an exact second-order fit could not cleanly attribute it to $\Delta_n$.

### 8.2 The algorithm

Eq. (E1) gives one frame's fit; `phase.methods.step_field.aia_step_field` runs it as an iterative
correction loop around the piston-model AIA solve:

1. **Initial solve.** Run `aia.md`'s piston-only algorithm (`aia`) to convergence, giving
   $(a, u, v, \delta_n, g_n)$ and, if gain is jointly fit, the per-frame offset $c_n$.
2. **Basis.** Build the orthonormal basis $p_1,\dots,p_J$ of degree $1..M$ on centered,
   unit-scaled coordinates, each zero-mean over the field (Eq. T3), $J=\tfrac{(M+1)(M+2)}{2}-1$
   (`_poly_basis`). $M=0$ gives $J=0$ and skips the remaining steps entirely — the plain `aia`
   result.
3. **Per-frame fit.** For every frame, compute $w_n = uQ_n-vP_n$ and the residual $r_n$ (§8.1,
   against $I_n-c_n$ when gain is jointly fit), then solve Eq. (E1) for $c_{\cdot n}$ and record
   its conditioning $\kappa_\Delta^{(n)}=\operatorname{cond}(G^{(n)})$ (`fit_step_field`).
4. **Score.** Rebuild each frame at the corrected step $\delta_n+\Delta_n(x,y)$ and take the
   residual RMS as a fraction of the data's own RMS, over a border-cropped region
   (`step_field_quality`). Keep this round's fields if its score is the best seen so far.
5. **Gauge-fix.** Before using $\{c_{jn}\}$ to correct the data, remove each basis term's frame
   mean, $c_{jn}\leftarrow c_{jn}-\bar c_j$ (Eq. T3b/E4, §8.4) — otherwise part of a real
   correction would be double-counted with $\Phi$.
6. **Correct and re-solve.** Add the fitted field back into the *original* stack, $I_n' = I_n +
   w_n\Delta_n(x,y)$ (Eq. E1 fits $r_n\approx-w_n\Delta_n$, so recovering it means adding, not
   subtracting) — from the original data each round, not a running buffer, since each round's fit
   estimates the total current step field, not an increment. Re-run the pixel step and frame step
   on $I'$: re-pin $\delta_1=0$, and, if gain is jointly fit, re-whiten $(u,v)$, renormalize
   $g_n$, and re-center $c_n$.
7. **Stop.** Repeat 3–6 until a round's non-negative improvement in the score falls below a
   tolerance, or a round budget is spent — then **report the best-scoring round**, not
   necessarily the last one run, since a round can make the fit worse.

In code this is `aia_step_field(stack, g, degree=M, refine_iters=..., refine_tol=..., crop=...)`.
`degree=0` or `refine_iters=0` both collapse to the plain `aia` result (`degree=1` is registered
separately as `"aia_tilt"`).

### 8.3 Conditioning

$G^{(n)}$ is a **$w_n^2$-weighted** Gram matrix of the basis functions, not their plain Gram
matrix — an orthonormal basis under the unweighted field inner product is not automatically
orthogonal under this weight. $w_n(x,y) = u(x,y)Q_n-v(x,y)P_n$ vanishes wherever $\Phi(x,y)+\delta_n$
crosses a node of $\sin(\cdot)$ — frame $n$'s fringe nulls — so a basis term whose shape
concentrates there contributes little to $G^{(n)}$ regardless of its plain-inner-product
conditioning. This is a genuinely new failure mode past $M=1$: two basis directions can be well
separated in the plain sense yet become nearly degenerate under a particular frame's $w_n^2$
weighting. $\kappa_\Delta^{(n)}$ (Eq. E3) flags this per frame — a large value means that frame's
fit to $c_{\cdot n}$ is unreliable even if the overall AIA solve reports low $\kappa_p,\kappa_{ps}$,
since it is a per-pixel-regression conditioning question, not the temporal one those two already
cover. $\max_n\kappa_\Delta^{(n)}$ (`kappa_fit` in `StepFieldParam`) is a reasonable single
summary; a sudden jump when $M$ is increased by one degree is a sign the added basis term is not
well resolved by the recorded fringe pattern, and a reason to stop increasing $M$ regardless of
what `step_field_quality`'s residual metric says.

$$\kappa_\Delta^{(n)} \equiv \operatorname{cond}\big(G^{(n)}\big) \tag{E3}$$

### 8.4 Gauge fixing

Eq. (E1) is solved independently per frame and has no mechanism to enforce the frame-mean-zero
gauge, Eq. (T3b) — nothing in a single frame's regression can know what the other frames'
coefficients are. The fitted $\{c_{jn}\}$ therefore generically carries a nonzero mean $\bar c_j
\equiv \langle c_{jn}\rangle_n$, which by Eq. (T3b)'s invariance is indistinguishable from (and
should be treated as) part of $\Phi$, not the step field: project it out before correcting the
data,

$$c_{jn} \;\leftarrow\; c_{jn} - \bar c_j, \qquad j=1,\dots,J \tag{E4}$$

and let the removed static part $\sum_j\bar c_j\,p_j(x,y)$ be picked up on the next pixel/frame-
step re-solve, the way any other static addition to the phase is. Skipping this step does not
change $I_n$ (Eq. T3b is an exact invariance) but leaves an ambiguity in how a real piece of
$\Phi$'s shape is reported — split unpredictably between the recovered $\Phi$ map and the
$\{c_{jn}\}$ diagnostics — and, across refinement rounds, can leave that split drifting instead
of settling.

## 9. Harmonic fingerprint and vanishing conditions

### 9.1 Discrete-Fourier framing and vanishing conditions

For the uniform step set $\delta_n = 2\pi n/N$ (`aia.md`'s recommended default, minimizing
$\kappa_{ps}$), $R_\Delta$, $R_\Delta^{(2)}$, and $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$ are, respectively,
the bin-1, bin-2, and bin-0 discrete Fourier components (in $n$) of the per-frame step-field
sequences $g_n\Delta_n$, $g_n^2\Delta_n$: bin 0 feeds the $\Phi$-independent ramp in $\Delta\Phi$,
bin 1 the $\Delta a$ ripple, bin 2 the $\Delta b$ and oscillatory-$\Delta\Phi$ ripple. This holds
for every basis term $j$ independently, since each $\rho_j,\rho_j^{(2)},\mu_j$ of Eq. (T8a) is
itself a bin-1/2/0 DFT coefficient of $g_nc_{jn}$, $g_n^2c_{jn}$.

By Eqs. (T17)–(T19), each bias vanishes iff its own moment does: $\Delta a\equiv0 \iff
R_\Delta=0$; the oscillatory part of $\Delta b$ and $\Delta\Phi$ vanish together iff
$R_\Delta^{(2)}=0$; the ramp in $\Delta\Phi$ vanishes iff $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle=0$. Since each
is a fixed combination of the per-order moments $\rho_j,\rho_j^{(2)},\mu_j$, the general vanishing
condition is $\rho_j=0$ (resp. $\rho_j^{(2)}=0$, $\mu_j=0$) for every $j=1,\dots,J$ — no single
symmetry nulls all three at once, and raising $M$ only adds more moments that must each vanish.

### 9.2 A frame-independent step field is harmless

If the step-field coefficients do not vary from frame to frame, $c_{jn}\equiv c_j$ for every $j$,
Eq. (T3b)'s gauge is violated maximally ($\bar c_j = c_j$) and by its invariance this is exactly
$\Delta_n(x,y)\equiv\Delta(x,y)=\sum_jc_jp_j(x,y)$ factoring out of every moment in Eq. (T8):

$$R_\Delta = \Delta(x,y)\,R, \qquad R_\Delta^{(2)} = \Delta(x,y)\,R^{(2)}, \qquad \langle g^2\Delta\rangle = \Delta(x,y)\,\langle g^2\rangle$$

Under the same good-coverage condition of §4.3, $R=R^{(2)}=0$ exactly, so $R_\Delta=R_\Delta^{(2)}=0$
as well: **a static step-field error, of any order, is invisible to this mechanism** — it is
exactly $\phi_{\text{carrier}}$ picking up an extra low-order term, removed together with it by
carrier fitting — and only the surviving $\langle g^2\Delta\rangle$ term feeds a proportional
addition into $\Delta\Phi$'s already-harmless ramp. It is specifically a step field that
**changes from frame to frame** that produces the $\Delta a$, $\Delta b$ fringes of §5–6. The
underlying reason (Eq. T3b's gauge invariance) applies identically at any $M$.

### 9.3 Worked case: a monotone drift

Take $g_n\equiv1$, the uniform step set $\delta_n=2\pi n/N$ ($n=0,\dots,N-1$), and a step field
that drifts linearly over the sweep, $c_{jn}=\kappa_j n$ for constants $\kappa_j$ — e.g. a
stepping actuator whose parasitic aberration accumulates with each step rather than resetting.
This is unrestricted in $M$: $p(x,y)$ can be the tilt basis, a quadratic basis, or any mix. Then
$\Delta_n(x,y) = n\Lambda(x,y)$ with $\Lambda(x,y) \equiv \sum_j\kappa_jp_j(x,y)$ any fixed,
zero-field-mean spatial pattern. With $\omega\equiv e^{i2\pi/N}$, using the identity
$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}n\,\omega^n = N/(\omega-1)$ for $\omega^N=1,\ \omega\ne1$:

$$R_\Delta = \Lambda\Big\langle n\,\omega^n\Big\rangle_n = \frac{\Lambda}{\omega-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|R_\Delta| = \frac{|\Lambda(x,y)|}{2\sin(\pi/N)} \tag{T23}$$

and, applying the same identity with $\omega\to\omega^2$,

$$\big|R_\Delta^{(2)}\big| = \frac{|\Lambda(x,y)|}{2\sin(2\pi/N)} \tag{T24}$$

Both amplitudes are set by $|\Lambda(x,y)|$ — the *total* excursion accumulated between the first
and last frame at that pixel — not by the number of frames averaging it down: for large $N$,
$\sin(\pi/N)\approx\pi/N$ so $|R_\Delta| \to N|\Lambda|/2\pi = (\text{total excursion})/2\pi$,
i.e. the bias saturates to a fixed fraction of the drift's total span rather than shrinking. A
systematic, monotone step-field drift does not average away by acquiring more frames, whatever
its spatial order.

## 11. Assumptions used

1. **Origin at the field centroid, each basis term zero-mean, and coefficients zero-frame-mean**
   (§1.2) — the first makes the piston/step-field split of Eq. (T1) exact; the second (Eq. T3b)
   is an exact gauge fixing an otherwise real ambiguity between $\Delta_n$ and $\Phi$, needed to
   define the per-frame estimator of §8 uniquely once $J>1$.
2. **Small step-field error, expansion to second order** (§2.2) — $\Delta_n$ is treated as small
   enough that $O(\Delta_n^3)$ terms are negligible.
3. **Piston $\delta_n$ held at its true value; only the pixel step is analyzed** (§1.3, §7) — the
   frame step's own sensitivity to $\Delta_n$ is argued, not derived, to be a smaller,
   higher-order effect under good coverage that degrades somewhat as $M$ grows.
4. **Good $\delta$/$g$ coverage for the diagonalization of $A_p$** (§4.3) — uniform steps and
   constant $g_n$, giving $R=R^{(2)}=0$ for $N\ge3$. Without it, $A_p$'s off-diagonal terms
   (Eq. T12) mix into $\Delta a,\Delta b,\Delta\Phi$ as an additional, unanalyzed source of bias,
   compounding a large $\kappa_p$.
5. **No noise** (§4.1) — the propagation isolates the deterministic step-field bias; measurement
   noise adds its own, independent term to Eq. (T10) and is not carried through this document.
6. **Truncation at degree $M$** (§1.1, §8) — the un-modeled degree-$>M$ remainder of the true
   step field biases the degree-$M$ fit exactly the way the full $\Delta_n$ biased the
   piston-only model, one level down; §5.6/§8.3's diagnostics are the practical check for whether
   that remainder still matters at the current $M$.
